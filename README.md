# UI-UX-Experiment5

## Aim:
The UX Design Experiment aims to address critical user experience issues in two real-world digital design contexts:

Food Delivery App Design: The goal is to develop a simple and user-friendly mobile application that helps users discover, compare, and order food with minimal effort. The app should reduce decision fatigue by providing clear information about prices, offers, delivery times, and restaurant options. It should also simplify the checkout process and provide accurate real-time order tracking.

College Learning & Assignment App Design: The goal is to create a centralized and user-friendly academic platform that helps students manage assignments, study materials, deadlines, and academic progress in one place. The app should reduce information scattered across multiple platforms, simplify navigation, provide effective reminders, and help students organize their studies and complete academic tasks on time.

## Algorithm:

1. Identify user pain points through interviews, surveys, and real-world observation.
2. Define UX problem statements based on specific user needs.
3. Choose suitable research methods:
Food Delivery App: User Interviews, Surveys, Usability Testing
College Learning & Assignment App: User Interviews, Surveys, Usability Testing
4. Create user personas to represent typical users and their goals.
5. Use insights to inform design improvements for usability, personalization, and accessibility.

# Scenario 1: Designing a Food Delivery Mobile App

## 1. Problem Statements

**● Problem Statement 1:**
“College students need a quick way to find affordable meals because food delivery apps often show too many choices and make it difficult to compare prices, delivery times, and offers, which results in decision fatigue and abandoned orders.”

**● Problem Statement 2:**
“Users need a reliable way to track their food orders because delivery updates are sometimes unclear or delayed, which creates uncertainty about when their food will arrive.”

## 2. Research Methods

**● User Interviews**
Interview students and working professionals about their food ordering habits, preferences, and frustrations.
**Justification:** Helps understand users’ decision-making process and expectations.

**● Surveys**
Collect information about ordering frequency, preferred features, payment methods, and common problems.
**Justification:** Helps identify patterns and priorities among a larger group of users.

**● Usability Testing**
Ask users to search for a restaurant, compare dishes, place an order, and track delivery.
**Justification:** Helps identify navigation difficulties and confusing interface elements.

## 3. Personas

### Persona 1: Karthik Anand

* **Demographics:** 21, Male, College Student, Chennai
* **Goals:** Find affordable food quickly and use the best offers
* **Behaviors:** Orders food several times a week, uses mobile, compares prices
* **Pain Points:** Too many options, hidden charges, confusing offers
* **Motivations:** Wants affordable food with fast delivery

### Persona 2: Sneha Kapoor

* **Demographics:** 27, Female, Marketing Executive, Bangalore
* **Goals:** Order healthy meals conveniently during work
* **Behaviors:** Orders during lunch breaks, prefers saved restaurants and quick reordering
* **Pain Points:** Long checkout process, inaccurate delivery estimates
* **Motivations:** Wants a convenient and predictable ordering experience

# Scenario 2: Designing a College Learning & Assignment App

## 1. Problem Statements

**● Problem Statement 1:**
“College students need a centralized way to manage assignments, notes, and deadlines because academic information is often scattered across multiple platforms, which results in missed deadlines and difficulty organizing their studies.”

**● Problem Statement 2:**
“Students need a simple way to track their academic progress because existing learning platforms provide information without clearly showing what they have completed and what still needs attention, which makes planning difficult.”

## 2. Research Methods

**● User Interviews**
Interview college students to understand how they currently manage assignments, notes, and deadlines.
**Justification:** Helps uncover real study habits and organizational challenges.

**● Surveys**
Collect data about students’ preferred learning features, notification preferences, and most common academic difficulties.
**Justification:** Helps identify common problems across different student groups.

**● Usability Testing**
Ask students to find an assignment, upload their work, check deadlines, and track completed tasks.
**Justification:** Reveals navigation problems and areas where the interface causes confusion.

## 3. Personas

### Persona 1: Varun Kumar

* **Demographics:** 20, Male, Engineering Student, Chennai
* **Goals:** Keep track of assignments and prepare for exams on time
* **Behaviors:** Uses smartphone frequently, checks deadlines at the last minute
* **Pain Points:** Forgets deadlines, information is spread across different apps
* **Motivations:** Wants everything related to college in one place

### Persona 2: Aishwarya Menon

* **Demographics:** 21, Female, Computer Science Student, Kochi
* **Goals:** Organize notes, assignments, and study schedules
* **Behaviors:** Plans her studies, uses digital notes, prefers visual progress tracking
* **Pain Points:** Difficult to find old materials, too many notifications
* **Motivations:** Wants an organized academic dashboard that helps her stay productive.
